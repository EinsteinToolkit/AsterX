\subsection{Special Behaviour}

\paragraph{Conservation across refinement boundaries.}
The conserved variables \texttt{cons\_vector} are updated with the divergence
of the face fluxes \texttt{flux\_x/y/z}, evaluated at every RK stage from
that stage's state inside \texttt{ODESolvers\_RHS}. On a multi-level grid the
coarse and fine levels disagree on the flux through a shared coarse/fine
face, and AsterX relies on one of two mechanisms, each in the regime where it
is natural, to keep mass, momentum and energy conserved across the interface:

\emph{Without subcycling} every level is at the same time inside
\texttt{ODESolvers\_RHS}. With \texttt{restrict\_fluxes = yes} (the
default) AsterX replaces the coarse fluxes on fine-covered faces by the fine
values before the coarse RHS uses them (\texttt{AsterX\_RestrictFluxes}), and
with \texttt{restrict\_aux\_terms = yes} (the default) it does the same for
the coarse gauge term $G$ on fine-covered vertices
(\texttt{AsterX\_RestrictAuxTermsForAvecPsiRHS}). The edge electric field $E$
is not restricted: it enters the \texttt{Avec} RHS only on the fine-covered
coarse edges themselves, which the driver's restriction of \texttt{Avec}
overwrites with the fine values.
Every RK stage is then conservative across the interface. CarpetX's flux
register (below), when enabled, is also fed and applied without subcycling,
at the end of each step, but with the fluxes already restricted the coarse
flux it accumulates is the fine one, so it holds only round-off and the
reflux has no effect; AsterX warns at \texttt{CCTK\_PARAMCHECK} if
\texttt{CarpetX::do\_reflux = yes} is set in this case, and leaving it at its
default (\texttt{no}) skips the register work. The two mechanisms
are not interchangeable here: with \texttt{restrict\_fluxes = no} and the
register alone, conservation is restored only at the end of the step, and
the intermediate coarse stage states next to the interface (which also feed
the prolongated fine ghost zones) carry the coarse interface flux.

\emph{With subcycling} the coarse level takes its step while the fine level
is outside the active window, so nothing can be restricted per stage and
\texttt{restrict\_fluxes} and \texttt{restrict\_aux\_terms} have no effect.
The conservative coupling is then provided by the CarpetX driver's flux register (\texttt{CarpetX::do\_reflux},
off by default, so it must be switched on), which \texttt{cons\_vector} opts into through the
\texttt{fluxes="flux\_x flux\_y flux\_z"} tag in \texttt{interface.ccl}.
ODESolvers feeds each RK stage's flux, weighted by the stage's effective
weight and the level's time step, into a register per level pair, and the
driver repays the accumulated coarse/fine mismatch to the coarse cells
adjacent to each refinement boundary once per coarse step, immediately
before it restricts the fine state onto the coarse level. The driver reads
the leading seven components of each flux group as the fluxes of
\texttt{dens}, \texttt{mom\{x,y,z\}}, \texttt{tau}, \texttt{DYe} and
\texttt{DEnt}; the trailing \texttt{f*B\{x,y,z\}} transport components,
which only feed the constrained-transport electric field, are ignored by the
register. AsterX has no parameter of its own for this: set
\texttt{CarpetX::do\_reflux = yes} to enable the correction; at its default
(\texttt{no}) the subcycled evolution keeps the pre-reflux behaviour, with
no conservative coupling of \texttt{cons\_vector} across the refinement
boundary.

\emph{The magnetic field needs no register.} \texttt{Bvec} is the curl of
the edge-centred vector potential \texttt{Avec}, so the cross-level quantity
that matters is the time integral of the edge electric field (plus the gauge
term) on the shared edges, not a face flux. The driver's edge restriction of
\texttt{Avec} at the end of every time-aligned cascade overwrites every
coarse edge under the fine box, the edges on the fine box's boundary faces
included, with the fine time integral, and runs at the same moment as the
hydro reflux, after it and before the coarse state is used again. The nodal
gauge register described below removes the gauge part of the mismatch that
survives on uncovered coarse edges.

\emph{Tests.} \texttt{Sound\_wave\_AMR\_SC\_reflux} is a subcycled two-level
sound wave on a fully periodic domain with a fixed refined box; with
\texttt{CarpetX::do\_reflux = yes}, the masked, volume-weighted \texttt{sum} reduction of \texttt{dens},
\texttt{momx}, \texttt{tau} and \texttt{DYe} (written by
\texttt{CarpetX::out\_norm\_vars}) is constant to round-off over the run
(relative drift $2\times10^{-16}$ to $2\times10^{-15}$), while with
\texttt{CarpetX::do\_reflux = no} the same totals drift by $4\times10^{-6}$
to $3\times10^{-3}$. \texttt{DEnt} is excluded from that statement by
construction: on every successful con2prim AsterX resets
$\texttt{DEnt} = \texttt{dens}\,\times\,\texttt{entropy}$, so its total is not
conserved even on a single level. A periodic multi-level Alfv\'en wave is not
usable for this measurement, because the seed's vector potential is linear in
$y$ and $z$ (a uniform $B_x$ has no periodic potential): with periodic
transverse boundaries the prolongation of \texttt{Avec} onto the fine level
straddles the wrap-around jump, and with open transverse boundaries the
totals leak through the boundary at the $10^{-7}$ level whatever the
interface treatment. \texttt{magTOV\_Z4c\_AMR\_SC\_reflux} runs the
subcycled magnetised TOV star of \texttt{magTOV\_Z4c\_AMR\_SC\_gauge\_register}
with \texttt{CarpetX::do\_reflux = yes} and pins the correction on realistic physics. It does
not pin the masked totals of \texttt{cons\_vector}: the atmosphere leaves
through the outer boundary there, so they are not a conservation diagnostic,
and the first moment of \texttt{DEnt} differs between arm64 and x86\_64
builds by more than the test tolerance. \texttt{magTOV\_Z4c\_AMR\_SC} and
\texttt{magTOV\_Z4c\_AMR\_SC\_gauge\_register} set
\texttt{CarpetX::do\_reflux = no} (the default) explicitly and pin the pre-reflux subcycled
path; \texttt{magTOV\_Z4c\_AMR} is not subcycled and pins the per-stage
restriction path. The interface $B$-shell metric of the gauge-register
paragraph below, measured on \texttt{magTOV\_Z4c\_AMR\_SC\_reflux}, is the
guardrail that the reflux correction leaves the magnetic field near the
interface no worse than the gauge register alone.

\paragraph{Nodal gauge register (\texttt{AsterX::gauge\_register}).}
In the generalized Lorenz gauge the vector potential receives the gauge term
$-\partial_i G$. Under CarpetX subcycling the coarse and fine levels
accumulate different time integrals of $G$ at the nodes they share, and the
driver's restriction of \texttt{Avec} leaves that mismatch as a spurious curl
(a $B$ shell) in the first coarse cell outside each refinement boundary. With
\texttt{gauge\_register = yes} (the default) AsterX evolves a vertex ledger
\texttt{IG} with $\dot{\texttt{IG}} = G$ on every level and, in CarpetX's
\texttt{CarpetX\_PreRestrict} group, immediately before each time-aligned
restriction, shifts the coarse \texttt{Avec} edges near each interface by the
edge-difference of $\texttt{IG}_\mathrm{fine} - \texttt{IG}_\mathrm{coarse}$
(a pure gauge transformation using the same finite-difference operator as the
$-\partial_i G$ term, at \texttt{mag\_correction\_order}) so both levels use
one gauge integral per shared node; the driver's own restriction then resets
the fine-owned coarse edges from the fine level and its halo refill
re-prolongates the fine \texttt{Avec} ghosts from the corrected coarse level.
The register never enters the RHS,
changes \texttt{Avec} only within one gauge-stencil width of a refinement
boundary, and is re-zeroed on every full cascade, so \texttt{IG} never holds
more than one coarse step's worth of $\int G\,dt$ on a multi-level run.

\emph{Activation.} The register is active only when all three of
\texttt{gauge\_register = yes}, \texttt{vector\_potential\_gauge =
"generalized Lorenz"} and \texttt{CarpetX::use\_subcycling = yes} hold.
Otherwise $\texttt{IG\_rhs} = 0$, none of the reconcile routines is
scheduled, and the run is bit-identical to one without the register (without
subcycling the mismatch is identically zero). Under subcycling CarpetX
requires \texttt{restrict\_during\_sync = no}, so it restricts explicitly and
\texttt{CarpetX\_PreRestrict} is always traversed when the register is
active. The register requires a CarpetX that provides the
\texttt{CarpetX\_PreRestrict} group; against one that does not, the group is
never traversed and the register is inert.

\emph{Regridding.} On a two-level run every regrid that modifies a level
happens at a time-aligned point, where the preceding full cascade has already
zeroed \texttt{IG} on every level; the ledger prolongated onto new fine points
is therefore exactly zero and exact, and the correction runs unchanged on the
next full cascade. (With three or more levels a regrid of the finest level at
a partial cascade prolongates a non-zero intermediate ledger; that one-time
interpolation residual is accepted.) On a subcycled run with a single level no
full cascade ever runs, so \texttt{IG} accumulates $\int G\,dt$ over the whole
run without being re-zeroed; this has no effect on \texttt{Avec}, since no
reconcile runs either.

\emph{Cost.} Storage for \texttt{IG}, \texttt{IG\_rhs} and the scratch
\texttt{IGr} is always allocated (ODESolvers requires storage on every
rhs-tagged group), \texttt{IG\_rhs} is written inside the \texttt{Psi} RHS
loop, and \texttt{IG} is synchronized with the other evolved variables after
every stage whether or not the register is active (the driver requires every
evolved group to be valid everywhere at the top of each iteration). Under
subcycling the driver also re-prolongates the refinement-boundary ghosts of
\texttt{IG} from the parent after every restriction, as it does for every
evolved group; \texttt{restrict="no"} only keeps it from restricting
\texttt{IG} itself. An inert run therefore still pays the ghost fills of one
vertex grid function, but no extra kernel and no reconcile. An active run
adds, per time-aligned pass, one nodal restriction and one ghost
synchronization of the scratch ledger \texttt{IGr} plus the local shift and
adopt kernels; the register issues no \texttt{Avec} transfer of its own,
since the driver's restriction and halo refill follow the shift.

\emph{Validation and tests.} On a two-level magnetised TOV run with
\texttt{mag\_correction\_order = 2}, the register reduces the interface
$B$-shell metric by a factor of about 2360 ($2.1\times10^{-14}$ to
$9.1\times10^{-18}$) and brings the subcycled interface profile onto the
non-subcycled one. Three regression tests cover the AMR configurations:
\texttt{magTOV\_Z4c\_AMR\_SC} runs subcycled with \texttt{gauge\_register = no}
and pins the subcycled path bit-for-bit against reference data that predate
the register;
\texttt{magTOV\_Z4c\_AMR\_SC\_gauge\_register} runs the same setup as
\texttt{magTOV\_Z4c\_AMR\_SC} with the register on and CarpetX poisoning at
its default, against reference data generated with the register on;
\texttt{magTOV\_Z4c\_AMR} is not subcycled, so the register is inert there.
